Budget context. This period spends $2,467/week against $516/week in the benchmark (4.8×), so the volume arrows (reach, impressions, clicks, leads) mostly track the budget. The rate and cost rows — CTR, CPC, CTL, lead rate, CPL — are the like-for-like read.
How to read the two columns. The left figure is the selected period. The right is the KPI benchmark: for volume metrics (impressions, clicks, leads, spend) it is the average per week over the benchmark range; for rate and cost metrics (CTR, CPM, CPC, CPL, CTL) it is the aggregate ratio across the whole benchmark — never the average of daily ratios, which skews badly when spend is uneven. Green always means better: for costs that is down; spend has no better direction, so its arrow stays grey. The arrow on a volume metric compares the period total against what the benchmark's weekly rate would have produced over the same number of weeks, so it stays like-for-like whatever period you pick.
Reach, frequency and cost per 1,000 people reached count people, which do not add up across days — someone who sees an ad on five days is one person, not five. So these are Meta's own deduplicated counts for the exact period (available for the preset ranges and whole calendar months), and their benchmark is a typical calendar month in the benchmark range. Their arrow appears only when the period is about a month long, because both climb naturally with a longer window.

Two different scopes, deliberately. Opens are available for ALL mail — they come from the tracking-pixel read event, so the top block covers automated mail as well as broadcasts. Clicks are only reported per campaign, so every click figure is scoped to the broadcasts and the automated volume is excluded from it. Open-to-click basis. The old Looker report showed 125–144% because it divided total clicks by unique opens. This uses unique clicks ÷ unique opens, which caps at 100%. Across all broadcasts that is 4.0% (the old basis would read 5.4%).
Engagement history starts May 2, 2026. Opens and clicks come from an event stream ActiveCampaign only serves 100 rows at a time, so earlier periods show sends without opens until the backfill reaches them. Send volume is complete for the whole range.

Conversion

the definition agreed 2026-07-27

No contacts are tagged as clients yet — so conversion reads zero, correctly

Conversion is defined as ActiveCampaign's Status field reading Client or Returning Client (agreed 2026-07-27). Right now every one of the 3,911 contacts is Lead or Listing Lead, so this is an empty metric rather than a broken one — it will populate as the team tags won business.

This replaces the old Colab join, which matched leads to a conversions spreadsheet on phone number and was silently returning zero matches, so the previous report's “Converted” filter showed nothing at all.

Until then the honest sales-readiness signal is an engaged lead — one that has opened at least one email — which is what the tiles above and the Cost per Engaged Lead figure use.
